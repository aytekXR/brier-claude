Phase 03 · Visual Brand Kit

Visual identity

A fintech-grade system tuned for traders and professional financial readers — and deliberately the opposite of crypto-startup neon. Trust, precision, and earned credibility, rendered in ink and tabular numbers.

3.1 · Logo & mark

The Register Mark

The mark is a registration target — the precision crosshair a printer or surveyor uses to locate an exact point. Inside a scorecard frame, a crosshair locates a single plotted point: one prediction, resolved and placed on the record. It reads as measurement and verification, never as a meme stamp.

Primary mark

Brier

Reversed (on navy)

Construction & clearspace

The mark is built on a unit grid. Minimum clearspace equals the cap-height of the wordmark on all sides. Never reproduce the mark below 20 px (digital) or 8 mm (print); below that, use the dot-and-crosshair glyph alone as a favicon.

Naming & the wordmark

Brier is set in Source Serif 4 SemiBold. The name is load-bearing strategy: the Brier score is the canonical academic measure of probabilistic-forecast accuracy. The brand is the metric — an instant signal of legitimacy to the journalists and quants whose endorsement seeds trust. Always capital “B,” never all-caps, never italic in the lockup.

The credential — “Brier Verified”

Brier Verified FAS 78 · Elite n = 124 resolved

The embeddable creator badge is the brand’s ad unit. It pairs the verified glyph with a live FAS in the band color. Display-only: the badge always links back to the analyst’s public Brier page, and the score it shows can never be paid up.

Usage

Do

  • Keep the dot in Brier Blue as the single point of color in an otherwise monochrome mark.
  • Use the reversed mark on the navy or any band color at full contrast.
  • Pair mark + wordmark for primary lockups; mark alone for avatars and favicons.

Don’t

  • Recolor the frame into gradients, neon, or “crypto” chrome.
  • Rotate, skew, add glows, or place the mark on a busy photo.
  • Add a checkmark, flame, rocket, or any emoji-grade decoration.
3.2 · Color system

Ink, paper, and one earned accent

The palette is a rating-agency palette: warm paper, cool ink, an institutional navy, and a single trustworthy blue for verification. Color is information, not decoration — saturation is reserved almost entirely for the FAS scale, where it signals an earned result.

Rationale. Navy carries institutional weight (Moody’s, Bloomberg) without the coldness of pure black. Brier Blue is a single, exact azure — trust and precision — used sparingly so it always means “verified / interactive.” Warm paper signals editorial authority and reads softer than stark white in long data sessions.

The FAS scale — a single calibrated accent ramp

The Falsifiable Accuracy Score (0–100) is the product’s heart, so it owns the only multi-hue ramp in the system. Equal-chroma steps run from a neutral “no-signal” ochre up to a confident teal-green. Low scores use a restrained clay, never a fire-red — Brier reports, it does not condemn.

3.3 · Typography

Three voices: authority, surface, data

An engineered, open-source type system. A serif for institutional authority, a precision grotesque for the product surface, and a monospace for every number, ticker, and receipt — so data always looks like data.

3.4 · Visual language

Design principles

P-01

Evidence on the surface

Never make a claim the interface can’t immediately back. A score is always one click from its receipt.

P-02

Boring on purpose

Restraint is the strategy. The legal posture depends on never looking like a callout account. No drama, no meme styling.

P-03

Data is the ornament

Tables, sparklines, and tabular numbers are the decoration. We don’t add graphics; we let the data carry the page.

P-04

Hairlines, not boxes

Structure with thin rules and whitespace, the way a financial broadsheet does. Heavy containers and drop-shadows are rare.

Iconography

A single line-icon set: 1.6 px stroke, square 24 px grid, rounded joins, no fills except the verification dot. Icons are functional wayfinding, never illustrative spot art.

Chart principles

  • One ink line per series; the dataset is the hero, not the chrome.
  • Three canonical markers: utterance (blue dot), target (dashed ochre), resolution (band-colored dot).
  • Built on TradingView Lightweight Charts — the idiom this audience already trusts.
  • No 3-D, no area-fill gradients, no dual axes. Tabular tooltips with sourced values.

Imagery & illustration

Brier has almost no illustration by design. The “hero image” is the data itself — a leaderboard, a receipt, a share card. Where a real asset is required (creator avatar, clip thumbnail, press photo), use the official embed or a clean photo. Placeholders are honest about what belongs there:

Motion & micro-interactions

Motion principles
  • Instrument, not toy. 120–200 ms, ease-out, small distances. Motion confirms state; it never performs.
  • Numbers count up to a settled value, like a meter coming to rest — never bounce.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion; end-states are always the base style.
Micro-interactions
  • Receipt player seeks to the offset with a subtle marker pulse — once.
  • Hover a claim row → its receipt link and status chip gain weight.
  • Focus rings are a 3 px Brier-Blue halo; the same blue means “interactive” everywhere.
  • Copy-a-receipt and dispute-submitted use a quiet check, not confetti.
